# 1. NexArm User Manual

## 1.1 Introduction

NexArm is an intelligent robotic arm designed for interactive learning and secondary development. The system package includes the main robotic arm, the WonderEcho Pro AI voice interaction box, a PS3 wireless controller, a card reader, and teaching accessories such as recognition cards, color blocks, and wooden blocks. It supports a wide range of intelligent applications, including arm motion control, voice interaction, object grasping, tag recognition, and material sorting. This platform provides an ideal experience for advanced robotics education and AI application development.

## 1.3 Robot Boot Instructions

<p id ="p1-3"></p>

This section covers learning about the robot boot state and verifying the functions of each module. After proceeding, subsequent sections can be visited to learn app control.

To connect a remote tool to connect the robot, further learn related applications, and view programs, please proceed to view the content under the directory [1.5 Development Environment Setup and Configuration](#p1-5).

### 1.3.1 Precautions Before Booting

> [!NOTE]
>
> **Before starting the robotic arm, be sure to use the G clamp to secure the robotic arm base to the desktop or a stable platform. The robotic arm will generate swing and inertia during startup and operation. If not secured, this may cause the device to tip over, fall, or be damaged, and may also pose a safety risk.**

1. Connect the 12 V 5 A power adapter to the ESP32 controller. Performing this sequence ensures that all hardware components of the robotic arm operate normally.

    <img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image2.png" style="width:500px"  />

2. Do not stack robotic arms to avoid a blocked state upon booting, which may damage the servos.

3. Also, do not keep the robotic arm under heavy load for a long time, as this will also shorten the service life of the servos or directly damage them.

4. Maintain a safe clearance distance from the robotic arm prior to startup to prevent physical contact and personal injury.

### 1.3.2 Boot State Instructions

1. Push the switch to the **ON** gear.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image3.png" style="width:500px"  />

2. Wait for about 1 minute. The blue LED light LED1 on the expansion board will light up and keep flashing. The robotic arm returns to its initial posture, and the **buzzer** sounds a short beep, indicating that the device has finished booting.

> [!NOTE]
> **If the buzzer does not sound a short beep, there may be a problem with hardware such as the buzzer. This usually does not happen under normal use. If the above problem occurs, please contact customer service personnel for processing.**

3. The device defaults to AP direct connection mode upon leaving the factory. After successful booting, a hotspot starting with **HW** will be generated. The password **hiwonder** must be entered during app or remote desktop connection.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image4.png" style="width:800px"  />

> [!NOTE]
>
> * **If the device hotspot cannot be found after booting, troubleshooting can be done from the following:**
>
> * **Check according to the steps in this section of the tutorial Boot State Instructions.**
>
> * **If LED1 on the expansion board is blue and remains solidly lit, STA local area network mode might be set. First, press and hold the key1 button on the expansion board for 5 to 10 seconds. If the LED1 light starts to flash, a Wi-Fi starting with HW will be generated.**
>
> * **If the LED1 light does not flash after pressing and holding the key1 button, it may be because no SD card is detected. The SD card needs to be replugged.**
>
> * **If the expansion board light remains solid after being re-plugged, there may be an issue with the SD card, or the image was not flashed specifically for packages without a controller. It is recommended to replace the SD card or re-flash the image.**
>
> * **If it still cannot be started after replacing the SD card and re-burning the image, there may be a problem with the Raspberry Pi controller. It is recommended to contact customer service via email at support@hiwonder.com for further troubleshooting.**

After the robot boots up, the normal states of each module are shown in the following table for reference.

| Module | Verification Step | Phenomenon |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Expansion board LED | Observe LED lighting and flashing conditions | The factory default is AP direct connection mode. The LED is blue and flashes, indicating that network service configuration is complete. |
| Buzzer | Verify short beep | The buzzer sounds a short beep, representing that the onboard hardware of the expansion board is normal. |
| Button KEY1 on expansion board | Switch network state | After connecting to STA local area network mode via app, press and hold the KEY1 button to see if the LED1 indicator light flashes |
| Depth camera and robotic arm | 1. Open the app and connect the robot.<br />2. Open the **Robot Control** function to view the current return image of the depth camera.<br />3. Bring up the robotic arm control buttons and drag each joint servo in turn. | Displays real-time return image and rotates |

### 1.4.2 Connection Mode Introduction

The following are two network modes of the robot:

1. **AP Direct Connection Mode**: The controller creates a local Wi-Fi hotspot for mobile devices and PCs to connect directly, but does not provide internet access.
2. **STA LAN Mode**: The controller connects to a designated Wi-Fi network or hotspot, enabling local network and internet access.

**The robot boots up in AP direct connection mode by default. Application functions remain consistent regardless of whether AP direct connection or STA local area network mode is selected.**

> [!NOTE]
>
> * **The robotic arm must be secured firmly before starting. Independent robotic arms must use the G clamp to secure the base, and sandbox kits must ensure base screws are tightened to avoid tipping, falling, or damage caused by swing and inertia during operation.**
>
> * **It is recommended to first learn the configuration method of direct connection mode to experience corresponding functions. Local area network mode can be reviewed as needed.**

### 1.7.2 Function Introduction

#### 1.7.2.1 PC Software Connection

After the NexArm PC software starts, it will automatically scan and connect to the device serial port, and attempt to take over the ROS control channel. Whether the connection is successful can be judged by the status bar in the lower left corner of the interface.

When the connection is successful, the **ROS Receive Right** status indicator light in the lower left corner will turn from red to green, and the status text will display **Qt Takeover**. This indicates that the PC software has successfully connected to the control board and obtained current control authority. At this time, control instructions such as servos, coordinates, peripherals, and system parameters can be issued normally.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_12/media/image1.png" style="width:300px"  />

When the connection is unsuccessful, the status indicator light remains red, and the status text displays **Not Taken Over**. This indicates that effective communication has not yet been established. It may be due to unrecognized serial ports, device not powered on, USB not connected, serial port occupied by other programs, or ROS underlying control services not releasing control correctly.

After a successful connection, further click **Read Firmware Version**, **Read Battery Voltage**, or **Read Position** for communication verification. If the interface returns corresponding data, the communication link between the PC software and the device is normal.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_7/media/image3.png" style="width:1200px"  />

**Action Group Control** is mainly used to compile actions, save actions, and run actions. Centered on the positions of servos 1 to 6, it saves multiple postures of the robotic arm sequentially into action groups frame by frame. This is suitable for creating fixed-process actions, such as grasping, transporting, placing, and demonstrating actions. Each servo position can be adjusted in real-time, action frames can be added, and running times for each frame can be set. Then, running online via computer or downloading to the control board for onboard operation can be selected.

The controller adopts ESP-WROOM and AT32 dual-chip architecture design, deeply optimizing multi-degree-of-freedom underlying control logic, and easily sustaining continuous interpolation calculations and smooth acceleration/deceleration control. The motherboard utilizes 12 V 5 A high-power power supply and supports Type-C 5 V 5 A robust output, fully equipped with four-channel bus servo interfaces. It also integrates massive interaction resources such as native Wi-Fi/Bluetooth dual mode, a 0.96-inch OLED screen, dual custom buttons, and a three-channel I2C, providing a hardcore hub with powerful computing capability, intuitive interaction, and excellent expandability for robotics projects.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_9/media/image1.png" style="width:800px"  />

* **Control Parameters**

| Parameter | Description | Parameter | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Main chip model** | ESP-WROOM | **Main frequency** | ≤240 MHz |
| **Architecture** | 32-bit dual-core Xtensa LX6 | **Flash** | 4 MB |
| **ROM** | 448 KB | **SRAM** | 520 KB |
| **Slave chip model** | AT32F421F8P7 | **Main frequency** | ≤120 MHz |
| **Architecture** | 32-bit ARM Cortex-M4 | **Flash** | 64 KB |
| **Wi-Fi/Bluetooth** | Supported | **Communication method** | UART, I2C, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth |
| **Peripheral resources** | Wi-Fi/Bluetooth, 12-bit ADC, UART, I2C, 64-bit general timers x4, 64-bit high-precision timer | **Interfaces** | Type-C, UART serial port, I2C, bus servo, screen plus button interface |
| **Development environment** | Arduino IDE | **OLED screen** | 0.96 inch 128 by 64 pixels |
| **Custom buttons** | 2 | **Bus servo interfaces** | 2-channel 5264, 2-channel PH2.0 |
| **Buzzer** | PWM control frequency output | **I2C interfaces** | 3 channels |
| **Power output interface** | Type-C 5 V 5 A | **Power supply voltage** | 12 V 5 A |

* **Power Supply Instructions**

A **12 V 5 A power adapter** is used to supply 12 V power to the ESP32 controller via the 12 V power input.
