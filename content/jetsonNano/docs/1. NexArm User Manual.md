## 1.3 Robot Boot Instructions

<p id ="p1-3"></p>

This section covers learning about the robot boot state and verifying the functions of each module. After proceeding, subsequent sections can be visited to learn app control.

To connect a remote tool to the robot, further learn related applications, and view programs, please proceed to view the content under the directory [1.5 Development Environment Setup and Configuration](#p1-5).

### 1.3.1 Precautions Before Booting

> [!NOTE]
>
> **Before starting the robotic arm, be sure to use the G clamp to secure the robotic arm base to the desktop or a stable platform. The robotic arm will generate swing and inertia during startup and operation. If not secured, this may cause the device to tip over, fall, or be damaged, and may also pose a safety risk.**

1. Connect the 12 V 5 A power adapter to the ESP32 controller. Performing this sequence ensures that all hardware components of the robotic arm operate normally.

    <img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image1.png" style="width:500px"  />

2. Do not stack robotic arms to avoid a blocked state upon booting, which may damage the servos.

3. Do not keep the robotic arm under heavy load for extended periods, as this can shorten the servos' service life or directly damage them.

4. Maintain a safe clearance distance from the robotic arm prior to startup to prevent physical contact and personal injury.

### 1.3.2 Boot State Instructions

1. Push the switch to the **ON** gear.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image2.png" style="width:500px"  />

2. Wait for about 1 minute. The blue LED1 on the expansion board will light up and keep flashing. The robotic arm returns to its initial posture, and the **buzzer** sounds a short beep, indicating that the device has finished booting.

> [!NOTE]
> **If the buzzer does not sound a short beep, there may be a problem with hardware such as the buzzer. This usually does not happen under normal use. If the above problem occurs, please contact customer service personnel for processing.**

3. The device defaults to AP direct connection mode upon leaving the factory. After successful booting, a hotspot starting with **HW** will be generated. The password **hiwonder** must be entered during app or remote desktop connection.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image3.png" style="width:800px"  />

> [!NOTE]
>
> * **If the device hotspot cannot be found after booting, troubleshooting can be done as follows:**
>
> * **Check according to the steps in this section of the tutorial Boot State Instructions.**
>
> * **If LED1 on the expansion board is blue and remains solidly lit, STA local area network mode might be set. First, press and hold the key1 button on the expansion board for 5 to 10 seconds. If the LED1 light starts to flash, a Wi-Fi starting with HW will be generated.**
>
> * **If the LED1 light does not flash after pressing and holding the key1 button, it may be because no SD card is detected. The SD card needs to be reinserted.**
>
> * **If the expansion board light remains solid after being re-plugged, there may be an issue with the SD card, or the image was not flashed specifically for packages without a controller. It is recommended to replace the SD card or re-flash the image.**
>
> * **If it still cannot be started after replacing the SD card and re-burning the image, there may be a problem with the Raspberry Pi controller. It is recommended to contact customer service via email at support@hiwonder.com for further troubleshooting.**

After the robot boots up, the normal states of each module are shown in the following table for reference.

| Module | Verification Step | Phenomenon |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Expansion board LED | Observe LED lighting and flashing conditions | The factory default is AP direct connection mode. The LED is blue and flashes, indicating that network service configuration is complete. |
| Buzzer | Verify a short beep | The buzzer sounds a short beep, representing that the onboard hardware of the expansion board is normal. |
| Button KEY1 on expansion board | Switch network state | After connecting to STA local area network mode via app, press and hold the KEY1 button to see if the LED1 indicator light flashes |
| Depth camera and robotic arm | 1. Open the app and connect the robot.<br />2. Open the **Robot Control** function to view the current return image of the depth camera.<br />3. Bring up the robotic arm control buttons and drag each joint servo in turn. | Displays the real-time return image and rotates |

### 1.4.2 Connection Mode Introduction

The following are two network modes of the robot:

1. **AP Direct Connection Mode**: The controller creates a local Wi-Fi hotspot for mobile devices and PCs to connect directly, but does not provide internet access.
2. **STA LAN Mode**: The controller connects to a designated Wi-Fi network or hotspot, enabling local network and internet access.

**The robot boots up in AP direct connection mode by default. Application functions remain consistent regardless of whether AP direct connection or STA local area network mode is selected.**

> [!NOTE]
>
> * **The robotic arm must be secured firmly before starting. Independent robotic arms must use the G clamp to secure the base, and sandbox kits must ensure base screws are tightened to avoid tipping, falling, or damage caused by swing and inertia during operation.**
>
> * **It is recommended to first learn the configuration method of direct connection mode to experience corresponding functions. Local area network mode can be reviewed as needed.**

#### 1.4.2.1 Direct Connection Mode - Required

**Requires iOS 11.0 or later and Android 5.0 or later.**

Special attention is needed for Android devices. Please be sure to grant all permissions to the app in the phone settings, otherwise normal function implementation may be affected.

### 1.5.1 Remote Tool Introduction and Installation

#### 1.5.1.1 Remote Tool Introduction

Regarding the two methods for remote robot control: graphical control and command-line control.

NoMachine is graphical remote desktop software. After installation, connecting to the robot's hotspot enables direct control of the robot on a computer. Specifically, the **Jetson Nano controller uses NoMachine** for remote access. This software provides a clear display of the robot's system desktop for intuitive visual operation.

Compared to NoMachine, MobaXterm establishes an SSH connection focused on command-line control. It does not render the full desktop of the robot system, displaying only a command terminal window. Operating via the command line allows faster robot control while reducing computational overhead and memory consumption.

MobaXterm includes a built-in lightweight X11 server capable of directly displaying graphical application windows. The SSH connection method provided by MobaXterm applies to all controller versions.

In summary, NoMachine is suitable for scenarios requiring intuitive visual operation, whereas MobaXterm is ideal for rapid command execution. Select the appropriate software according to specific operational requirements.

**STA LAN Mode: The development board can proactively connect to a specified hotspot/Wi-Fi router that is accessible to external networks.**

> [!NOTE]

2. Enter the command and press **Enter** to navigate to the configuration file directory:

```
cd wifi_manager
```

3. Enter the command and press **Enter** to open the configuration file:

```
gedit wifi_conf.py
```

4. First, modify the value of `WIFI_MODE` to `2`. Here, `1` represents direct connection mode, where the robot autonomously generates Wi-Fi, and `2` represents LAN mode, where the robot connects to the local Wi-Fi router network. Set this parameter to `2` here.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_5/media/image30.png" style="width:800px"  />

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_5/media/image31.png" style="width:800px"  />

6. After verifying the input is correct, press the combination key **Ctrl + S** to save, and then click the close button in the upper right corner.

7. Return to the terminal and enter the command to restart the robot Wi-Fi service:

```
sudo systemctl restart wifi.service
```

8. To switch back to direct connection mode, re-edit this configuration file, change `WIFI_MODE` back to 1, and restart the Wi-Fi service following the previous step.

## 1.6 Color Threshold Adjustment

Under different light sources, the presentation effect of object colors varies, and this phenomenon will have a certain impact on application functions involving color recognition.

> [!NOTE]
>
> **Before starting the robotic arm, be sure to use the G clamp to secure the robotic arm base to the desktop or a stable platform. The robotic arm will generate swing and inertia during startup and operation. If not secured, this may cause the device to tip over, fall, or be damaged, and may also pose a safety risk.**

### 1.6.1 App Color Threshold Adjustment

To address this problem, this section will cover how to perform color threshold adjustment within the app. The **Android app** is used here as an example. This process applies to iOS devices as well.

1. Start the robotic arm and connect it to the **WonderROS** app. For details on booting and starting the robotic arm, refer to [1.3 Robot Boot Instructions](#p1-3) and [1.4 App Control](#p1-4) above.

   <img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_6/media/image1.jpg" style="width:800px"  />

2. On the interactive mode selection interface, tap the icon <img class="inline-icon" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_6/media/image2.png" style="width:70px" /> in the upper right corner of the interface to enter the color threshold adjustment interface.

3. Taking red as an example, place the red object within the visual field of the camera and select red in **Select Color**.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_6/media/image3.png" style="width:800px"  />

4. Tap the **Instruction** button at the bottom right of the interface to view the color threshold adjustment tutorial.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_6/media/image4.png" style="width:800px"  />
<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_6/media/image5.png" style="width:800px"  />

5. Set all **min** values of the L, A, and B components to **0**, and all **max** values to **255**.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_6/media/image6.png" style="width:600px"  />

6. Refer to the LAB color space distribution diagram and adjust the L, A, and B components towards the interval of the target recognition color.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_6/media/image7.png" style="width:600px"  />

Red is close to **+a**, so the A component needs to be increased, meaning the **L_max** value remains unchanged, and the **L_min** value increases until the color object area in the image on the left side of the image display area turns white, and other areas turn black.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_6/media/image8.png" style="width:800px"  />

7. Adjust the remaining L and B components according to the surrounding environment. If the red is lighter, increase the **L_min** value. If darker, decrease the **L_max** value. If the red is warmer, increase the **b_min** value. If cooler, decrease the **b_max** value.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_6/media/image9.png" style="width:800px"  />

8. After adjustment is complete, tap the **Save** button at the bottom right of the interface to save the adjusted color thresholds.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_6/media/image10.png" style="width:800px"  />

After the NexArm PC software starts, it will automatically scan and connect to the device's serial port and attempt to take over the ROS control channel. Whether the connection is successful can be judged by the status bar in the lower-left corner of the interface.

This indicates that the PC software has successfully connected to the controller and obtained current control authority. It may be due to unrecognized serial ports, the device not being powered on, the USB not being connected, the serial port being occupied by other programs, or ROS underlying control services not releasing control correctly.

After a successful connection, click **Read Firmware Version**, **Read Battery Voltage**, or **Read Position** for communication. If the interface returns the corresponding data, the communication link between the PC software and the device is normal.

## 1.10 Hardware Instructions

This chapter mainly introduces ROS robot hardware configurations, including electronic control systems and visual modules.

### 1.10.1 Electronic Control System Introduction

* **ESP32 Core Board**

The controller adopts ESP-WROOM and AT32 dual-chip architecture design, deeply optimizing multi-degree-of-freedom underlying control logic, and easily sustaining continuous interpolation calculations and smooth acceleration/deceleration control. The controller utilizes 12 V 5 A high-power power supply and supports Type-C 5 V 5 A robust output, fully equipped with four-channel bus servo interfaces. It also integrates massive interaction resources such as native Wi-Fi/Bluetooth dual mode, a 0.96-inch OLED screen, dual custom buttons, and a three-channel I2C, providing a hardcore hub with powerful computing capability, intuitive interaction, and excellent expandability for robotics projects.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_10/media/image1.png" style="width:800px"  />

* **Control Parameters**

| Parameter | Description | Parameter | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Main chip model** | ESP-WROOM | **Main frequency** | ≤240 MHz |
| **Architecture** | 32-bit dual-core Xtensa LX6 | **Flash** | 4 MB |
| **ROM** | 448 KB | **SRAM** | 520 KB |
| **Slave chip model** | AT32F421F8P7 | **Main frequency** | ≤120 MHz |
| **Architecture** | 32-bit ARM Cortex-M4 | **Flash** | 64 KB |
| **Wi-Fi/Bluetooth** | Supported | **Communication method** | UART, I2C, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth |
| **Peripheral resources** | Wi-Fi/Bluetooth, 12-bit ADC, UART, I2C, 64-bit general timers x4, 64-bit high-precision timer | **Interfaces** | Type-C, UART serial port, I2C, bus servo, screen plus button interface |
| **Development environment** | Arduino IDE | **OLED screen** | 0.96 inch 128 by 64 pixels |
| **Custom buttons** | 2 | **Bus servo interfaces** | 2-channel 5264, 2-channel PH2.0 |
| **Buzzer** | PWM control frequency output | **I2C interfaces** | 3 channels |
| **Power output interface** | Type-C 5 V 5 A | **Power supply voltage** | 12 V 5 A |
